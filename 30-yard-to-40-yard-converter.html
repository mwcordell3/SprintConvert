30 Yard to 40 Yard Dash Converter

Convert a 30-yard sprint time into an estimated 40-yard dash time. Useful for athletes whose training facility only timed the 30, or for spot-checking against a known 40.

Many football and field-sport athletes are timed at 30 yards in training but are reported at 40 yards in showcases and combines. The two are closely related — the last 10 yards mostly reflect transition into max velocity — so a 30 can give a reasonable 40 estimate when paired with a consistent start type and timing method.

Enter your 30-yard time and the tool will estimate every other distance from 10 yards through 200 meters, plus average speed and a general benchmark tier.

How the conversion works

  • Your 30-yard time is normalized to a fully-automatic, static-start baseline.
  • It is then scaled to 40 yards using reference proportions. The last 10 yards is roughly 1.05 times the 30-yard portion of the 40 for a competitive athlete.
  • Adjustments are applied for hand timing (+0.24s) and rolling/flying starts.
  • The confidence rating drops if your inputs alone don't capture the start phase well (for example, a flying 30).

Frequently asked questions

Is a 30-yard time really a good predictor of a 40?

It's better than nothing and useful for spot-checks, but it's not perfect. The last 10 yards of a 40 emphasize max velocity more than the first 30, and athletes with strong top-end speed will out-perform an extrapolation from their 30 alone.

What if my 30-yard split came from a longer run?

If you ran a full 40 and recorded a 30-yard split along the way, that's slightly different from a stand-alone 30-yard run. The estimate is still useful, but consider entering both the split and the full distance for a stronger result.

Can I do the reverse — 40 to 30?

Yes. Use the main calculator and enter your 40-yard time; the result table includes an estimated 30-yard split.
