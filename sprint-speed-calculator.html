Sprint Speed Calculator

Estimate your average sprint speed in miles per hour, meters per second, and kilometers per hour, plus an estimated peak velocity and general performance context.

Average speed is simple — distance divided by time — but real sprinters spend most of a 100m at well above their average velocity, with the peak hitting somewhere between 50 and 70 meters. This calculator returns both the average and an estimated peak, with the peak relying more heavily on optional inputs like a measured top speed or a flying-segment time.

How the speed numbers are calculated

  • Average speed = the input distance divided by the normalized time.
  • Estimated top speed uses a flying 10m or 20m if provided, then a measured top-speed input, then a fallback that scales average speed by typical max-velocity ratios for the input distance.
  • Conversions: 1 m/s = 2.237 mph = 3.6 km/h.

Estimated top speeds without a flying segment or a measured input are rough; treat the value as approximate.

Frequently asked questions

What's a good sprint speed in mph for a high-school athlete?

Top sprint speeds for high-school and college athletes typically fall between 18 and 23 mph, with elite track sprinters peaking above 25 mph. These are general ranges, not percentiles.

Why does my estimated top speed look slow for short distances?

The shorter the input distance, the less of the max-velocity phase you actually ran. The fallback estimator amplifies the average to compensate, but a measured value or a flying 20m time will give a much better number.

Can I enter my GPS-watch reading for top speed?

Yes. Use the optional Top speed field and pick the appropriate unit (mph or m/s). Sport GPS readings are typically within a couple of percent of true peak speed in open conditions.
