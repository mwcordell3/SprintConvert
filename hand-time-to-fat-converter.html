Hand Time to FAT Converter

Convert a hand-timed sprint to an estimated fully-automatic timing (FAT) equivalent. The classic +0.24-second adjustment is applied with full disclosure of its limits.

Hand stopwatches start later than electronic systems because of human reaction time. For track-style sprints (60m, 100m, 200m) the standard estimated adjustment is to add 0.24 seconds when converting hand to FAT. For short combine-style tests (40 yards), timing systems and starting cues vary more, so the real difference may be anywhere from 0.10 to 0.30 seconds.

This calculator applies the +0.24-second adjustment automatically when you choose the "Hand timed" option, or when you tick the manual hand-time adjustment box. The result is an estimate — not an official conversion.

What the +0.24s adjustment is, and isn't

  • It is a long-standing average adjustment used for track hand-times to estimate a FAT equivalent.
  • It assumes a stationary timer reacting to the gun or starting motion. Phone video, helper-with-stopwatch, and combine timing all behave a bit differently.
  • It is not an official conversion — never present a converted time as the athlete's official mark.
  • Real differences for the same effort have been measured anywhere from 0.10s to 0.30s depending on technique and equipment.

Frequently asked questions

Why exactly +0.24 seconds?

It is the long-standing estimated average for sprint hand times, used by various track organizations as an approximation. The actual delay varies between timers and conditions.

Is the adjustment the same for a 40-yard dash and a 100m?

No. Track hand times use the +0.24 convention. Combine hand times can vary more because of the start-cue technique used. Treat any conversion in either direction as an estimate.

What if my time was a phone video estimate?

Phone video timing is typically less accurate than a trained hand timer because of frame-rate uncertainty and start detection. The calculator marks phone-video inputs as low confidence regardless of distance.
