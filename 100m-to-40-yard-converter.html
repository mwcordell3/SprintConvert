100m to 40 Yard Dash Converter

Estimate a 40-yard dash time from a known 100-meter sprint. Helpful for track athletes exploring football testing, or for cross-comparing track and combine numbers.

The 100 meters and the 40 yards measure overlapping but different qualities. A 100m sprint includes a full max-velocity phase and some speed endurance; a 40-yard dash is dominated by acceleration. Converting between them is approximate — two athletes with identical 100m times can run notably different 40s depending on their start technique.

Why the 100m to 40 conversion is approximate

  • 100m times use block starts with FAT. 40-yard dashes typically use 3-point starts and may be hand-timed.
  • The 40-yard portion of a 100 is much faster than a stand-alone 40 because the athlete's reaction and start are already underway when the timer triggers.
  • The calculator estimates a "stand-alone" 40 from your 100 by reversing the static-start adjustment, but this is a directional estimate.

No guarantee is made that any estimate is accurate for a specific athlete.

Frequently asked questions

I ran 11.0 in the 100m. What 40 should I expect?

It depends on your start technique. Track athletes coming over to football testing often run slightly slower 40s at first because the 3-point start is unfamiliar. The calculator gives you a directional estimate — treat it as a starting point, not a guarantee.

Should I use a 100m split from a 200m as input?

If you only have a 200m time, use the main calculator with 200m as the distance — the engine handles speed-endurance scaling differently than a stand-alone 100.

Why is the confidence usually medium for this conversion?

We're crossing acceleration-heavy and max-velocity distances. Without splits or a measured top speed, the model can only scale proportionally.
