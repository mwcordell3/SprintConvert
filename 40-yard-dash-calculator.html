40 Yard Dash Calculator

Estimate your 40-yard dash time, sprint splits, average and top speed, and a general football performance tier. Educational tool — not an official combine result.

The 40-yard dash is the most common football speed test in the United States. Reported times depend heavily on the timing method used (hand stopwatch, laser, or fully automatic timing) and the start type (3-point versus standing). This calculator normalizes your input and shows estimated splits and conversions you can compare to general football ranges.

Use the optional fields to add a 10- or 20-yard split, a measured top speed, or a flying-segment time. Each one improves the confidence of the estimate.

Tips for a fair 40-yard test

  • Use the same timing method every test — comparing a hand-timed 40 to a laser-timed 40 is misleading.
  • Warm up. Cold-muscle 40s under-represent your real speed.
  • Test on a consistent surface (turf vs track vs grass changes results).
  • 3-point starts are the most common for football testing.
  • If you have phone-video timing, expect 0.10–0.30 seconds of error from frame rate and start detection.

This site provides estimated sprint conversions and general performance ranges for educational and training-reference purposes only. Results are not official marks, not exact percentiles, and should not be used as the sole basis for recruiting, scholarship, roster, medical, or training decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 4.5 forty good?

A 4.50 hand-timed 40 is typically slower than a 4.50 fully-automatic time because hand stopwatches start later. Either way, a 4.50 is in the elite range of our general high-school / college football benchmark. Treat the tier as broad guidance, not an exact percentile.

How is the calculator estimating my 100m time from a 40?

It scales your normalized 40 time using reference proportions for a competitive male athlete with FAT timing and a static start. Sprinting is not linear — short distances are acceleration-heavy and longer distances depend on max velocity and speed endurance — so the estimate becomes less precise the further the conversion goes.

Should I check the hand-time adjustment box?

Check it if your time was recorded by a person with a stopwatch and you want to estimate a fully-automatic equivalent. The +0.24-second adjustment is an estimate, not an exact conversion. For short combine-style tests timing systems vary and the real difference may be 0.10 to 0.30 seconds.

Why does the result include a confidence rating?

Sprint conversions get more reliable with more data. A single hand-timed run gets a low rating; a FAT run with a measured top speed and splits gets a high rating. The rating is shown so you can decide how much weight to give the estimate.
