How to get a cleaner 40-yard dash estimate

  • Use the same timing method every test. Mixing stopwatch, laser, and FAT results can mislead you.
  • Match the start type to the test. A standing start and a 3-point start are not the same performance.
  • Add a 10-yard split when possible. It gives the model a better read on acceleration.
  • Add measured top speed when you have GPS, laser, radar, or timing-gate data.
  • Test on a consistent surface. Turf, track, and grass can change the number.

This site provides estimated sprint conversions and general performance ranges for educational and training-reference purposes only. Results are not official marks, not exact percentiles, and should not be used as the sole basis for recruiting, scholarship, roster, medical, or training decisions.

FAQ

40-yard dash questions

Is a 4.5 forty good?

A 4.50 hand-timed 40 is typically slower than a 4.50 fully automatic time because hand stopwatches start later. Either way, a 4.50 is in the elite range of the general high-school and college football benchmark. Treat the tier as broad guidance, not an exact percentile.

How does SprintConvert estimate my 100m time from a 40?

It scales your normalized 40 time using non-linear sprint proportions. Short distances are acceleration-heavy and longer distances depend on max velocity and speed endurance, so the estimate gets stronger when you add splits or measured top speed.

Should I use the hand-time adjustment?

Use it if your time was recorded by a person with a stopwatch and you want a rough fully automatic timing equivalent. The +0.24-second adjustment is an estimate, not an official correction.

Why should I add splits or top speed?

A measured top speed and 10-yard or 20-yard splits help separate acceleration from max velocity, so the estimate is less generic and easier to sanity-check.
