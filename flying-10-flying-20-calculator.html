Flying 10 and Flying 20 Calculator

Estimate maximum sprint velocity from a flying 10m or 20m time. Flying segments isolate the max-velocity phase of sprinting after a build-up.

A flying 10 or flying 20 measures speed over a short segment after a running build-up. Because the athlete is already at high velocity when timing starts, the segment isolates max-velocity rather than acceleration. Coaches use it to track top-end speed across a training block.

Enter the segment time and the calculator returns peak velocity in mph, m/s, and km/h. Adding a stand-alone 40-yard or 100m time will let the engine cross-check the estimate.

Results

Reading flying-segment results

  • A flying 10m of 0.94 seconds is roughly 10.6 m/s (≈23.7 mph).
  • A flying 20m of 1.85 seconds is roughly 10.8 m/s (≈24.2 mph).
  • Wind, surface, footwear, and the build-up distance all change real values. Comparisons across sessions are most meaningful when conditions are similar.

This calculator does not predict a stand-alone 40-yard or 100-meter time directly from a flying segment alone — it provides directional estimates only.

Frequently asked questions

What's a good flying 20 time?

Trained sprinters often run flying 20m times around 1.95 to 1.85 seconds; elite sprinters break 1.80. Treat these as broad ranges, not exact percentiles.

Why is the confidence sometimes low for flying inputs?

Flying segments don't capture the start or acceleration phases, so converting a flying time to a stand-alone 40 (which is start-heavy) is a directional estimate at best.

Can I track progress with this?

Yes. Take measurements under similar conditions and compare top-speed values across sessions. Watch the change, not the absolute number.
